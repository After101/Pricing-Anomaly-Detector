# Pricing-Anomaly-Detector

Problem Statement
E-commerce shoppers often have to manually compare current prices with historical prices and prices of similar products to understand whether a product is unusually expensive or cheap. This becomes difficult when a product has limited or no price history. Our project addresses this by automatically analyzing the current price of headphones, earphones, and wireless earbuds using historical and category-level pricing patterns and providing a Pricing Anomaly Score that highlights prices that may require further investigation.

Our project is a Pricing Anomaly Detector for e-commerce headphones, earphones, and wireless earbuds. We use around 6,820 historical price observations from Amazon and Flipkart and first clean and organize the data. We then perform feature engineering by calculating things like the price compared to the median, price deviation, percentile, price variation, and category or brand pricing. These features are given to an Isolation Forest, which is our unsupervised machine-learning algorithm, to identify pricing patterns that are unusual compared with the data it has learned from. If a product has enough historical data, the system compares its current price with its own previous prices, while for new products it compares the price with other products in the same category. The model then produces a 0–1 Pricing Anomaly Score, where a higher score means the price is more unusual. Finally, we use streamlit to display the current price, anomaly score, pricing context, reasons behind the result, and relevant graphs on a website.
